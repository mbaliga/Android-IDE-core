Scenario (FB-RAT-AUTH-005 "time-bound" + task-brief-required "grant-expiry case... must deny, not
silently renew"):

fixtures/authority/valid/grant-ssh-remote-exec-now-expired-valid.json (grantId
grant-ssh-remote-exec-homepi-0009) declares expiresAtUtc="2026-07-01T00:00:00Z". This decision
record's decidedAtUtc is "2026-08-07T09:00:00Z" — over five weeks after that grant expired — yet
the request still cites it as matchedGrantId and the correct outcome is DENY.

Both timestamps individually validate fine against the `date-time` format keyword; JSON Schema
draft 2020-12 has no keyword that compares two `date-time` strings against each other (or against
"now") to determine which is later. That comparison — "is decidedAtUtc after this grant's
expiresAtUtc?" — is unavoidably a policy-engine runtime check, which is why a schema-valid record
showing the WRONG outcome (e.g. ALLOW) would sail through structural validation undetected.

Required real-implementation behavior:
- MUST compare the current decision instant against `Grant.expiresAtUtc` for every grant it
  resolves, on every check — not just once at grant-issuance time.
- MUST return outcome=DENY, reasonCode=AUTHORITY_GRANT_EXPIRED for any request evaluated at or
  after a matched grant's expiresAtUtc.
- MUST NOT silently renew, auto-extend, or treat an expired grant as still active "since nothing
  revoked it" — expiry is not equivalent to revocation and needs no separate revoke action to take
  effect; per grant.schema.json's own expiresAtUtc description, continuing access after expiry
  requires an entirely fresh Grant (new grantId, new expiresAtUtc), issued through the same
  explicit issuance path as the original, never an implicit side effect of a late-arriving request.
